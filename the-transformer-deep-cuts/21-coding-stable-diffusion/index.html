Coding Stable Diffusion from Scratch in PyTorch

A full latent diffusion stack: VAE Encoder/Decoder compress RGB to z; CLIP text tower yields context; U-Net with TimeEmbedding, ResidualBlock, and AttentionBlock cross-attention predicts ε; DDPMScheduler runs add_noise and step; classifier-free guidance mixes ε_u and ε_c; pipeline ties together text-to-image, img2img strength, and inpainting with a latent mask.

Latent diffusion: Markov chain in z

Pixel-space diffusion on 512×512×3 is costly. Latent diffusion runs the forward and reverse processes on z with shape [B,4,64,64] (typical 8× downsampling), then decodes once with the Decoder. The Encoder outputs μ and logσ²; reparameterization z = μ + σ⊙ε connects images to the noise space the U-Net sees.

Conditioning is a sequence of vectors from the CLIP token_embedding + positional_embedding stack, consumed as K,V in cross-attention. Training drops text randomly so the same weights support CFG at inference.

Public checkpoints trace to the 2022 CompVis latent-diffusion release. The Markov chain runs in compressed z, not full RGB—roughly O(H/8·W/8·4) per reverse step instead of O(H·W·3) at 512². VAE and DDPM foundations are in Chapter 19 and Chapter 20.

For standard text-to-image sampling, initialize z_T as Gaussian noise with shape [B,4,H/8,W/8]. The Encoder is used when pixels already exist—img2img, inpainting, or training-time encoding—not on freshly drawn noise.

q(z_t|z_0)=𝒩(√(α̅_t)z_0,(1−α̅_t)I)  ·  ε̃ = ε_u + w(ε_c − ε_u)

Weights. Keep scaling_factor on latents and tensor names (input_blocks, middle_block, output_blocks) aligned with the checkpoint you load via load_state_dict.

Encoder: conv, GroupNorm, downsample, μ/logvar

Stack ResidualBlock stages with SelfAttention at low resolution; halve H,W via stride or Downsample. The head Conv2d emits 2·z_ch channels split into mean and log_variance for the VAE posterior.

Channel–resolution ladder (512×512 RGB). A typical SD VAE encoder mirrors standard U-shaped CNN practice: Conv2d(3→128, k=3, pad=1) preserves 512×512; subsequent stride-2 convolutions (often with asymmetric reflect padding on bottom/right only when official implementations require aligned downsampling) halve spatial size while widening channels through stages such as 128 → 256 → 512. At H/8×W/8 (for 512 inputs, 64×64), self-attention mixes long-range spatial dependencies—treating flattened pixels as a sequence—without further shrinking the grid. A late 1×1 convolution can sit at the bottleneck so each spatial location keeps a compact code before splitting into μ and log σ² with four channels each when z_ch=4.

Convolution refresher. A kernel slides over local neighborhoods; products with kernel weights are summed into one output activation, so each location aggregates nearby context. Larger kernels or dilations widen the receptive field; stride > 1 subsamples the grid and reduces H,W. padding compensates border shrinkage when you need same spatial size after k=3 convolutions.

VAE posterior and sampling. The tensor from the last conv is chunk(..., 2, dim=1) into mean and log_variance. log_variance is often clamped before exponentiation so scales stay numerically sane; then std = exp(0.5 * logvar) and z = mean + std ⊙ ε with ε ~ 𝒩(0,I) (reparameterization). Many checkpoints also multiply latents by a fixed scaling factor read from the released config (commonly on the order of 0.18215 in public SD1.x assets) so diffusion training dynamics match the pretrained U-Net; treat it as part of the weight recipe, not a quantity to re-derive each time.

Deterministic bottleneck

Maps each image to a single code vector. Interpolation in that space is not guaranteed to be semantically smooth.

Variational bottleneck

Outputs Gaussian μ and log σ² per location; decoding uses a sample, shaping a latent manifold for downstream diffusion (see Chapter 19).

Sampling z

std = exp(0.5*logvar) keeps σ positive. Latents may be scaled by a constant scaling_factor before the U-Net to match pretrained dynamics.

Decoder: upsample, residual, GroupNorm, tanh

Mirror the encoder: conv_in from z_ch to wide channels, MidBlock with attention, Upsample stages, then GroupNorm+SiLU+Conv2d to 3 channels. Skip connections from the encoder (in a full AutoencoderKL) concatenate into decoder blocks.

Zip-file analogy. Think of the encoder as lossy compression: pixels go “into the zip” as a small latent grid; the decoder “unzips” back to RGB. Diffusion then edits files inside the zip—cheaper than diffusing full-resolution tensors.

Typical width progression. Latents enter at 4×(H/8)×(W/8), expand to 512 channels at the bottleneck width, pass residual and attention stacks, then each Upsample(scale=2) doubles H,W (nearest-neighbor expand plus a blur conv in many implementations) while residual stages may reduce channel counts (for example toward 256, then 128) as resolution grows. A final GroupNorm(32, 128) (thirty-two groups splitting one-hundred-twenty-eight channels into four groups of thirty-two statistics each) precedes Conv2d(128→3) and tanh so outputs live in a bounded range suited to [-1,1] image tensors.

If training introduced a constant multiplier on z before the U-Net, apply the inverse before feeding the VAE decoder so internal activations land in the domain the decoder weights expect.

Output range

Many checkpoints assume image tensors in [-1,1] before Encoder and after Decoder; map to uint8 for display with (x+1)*127.5.

04

CLIP text tower: Embedding, positions, causal layers

String → BPE token_ids length 77. token_embedding and learned positional_embedding sum to form the input to L transformer layers (768 dim, 12 heads typical). Each CLIPLayer applies causal self-attention then MLP with quick_gelu; output context drives U-Net cross-attention.

CLIP pretraining picture. Image–text pairs build a similarity matrix between image embeddings and caption embeddings; training pushes diagonal entries (matching pairs) high and off-diagonals low. Stable Diffusion checkpoints reuse only the text tower as a frozen conditioner: prompts become a tensor context ∈ ℝ^{B×77×768}.

Shapes for SD1.x–style text encoders are fixed by the published weights: vocabulary size 49 408, context length 77, hidden size 768, and 12 transformer layers (each with multi-head self-attention over tokens). Positional information uses learned position vectors added to token embeddings—not the sinusoidal table from the original “Attention Is All You Need” encoder—because the CLIP recipe found that workable and simpler to ship with weights.

quick_gelu(x) = x · σ(1.702 · x)

The MLP nonlinearity above is the Quick GELU approximation used in OpenAI’s CLIP code path; it substitutes for exact GELU in many ports. SiLU / Swish (x·σ(x)) appears widely in convolutional residual blocks; naming differs by paper and codebase even when curves are similar.

Mask

Lower-triangular attention mask matches left-to-right training; pad tokens can be masked out of softmax for variable-length prompts.

TimeEmbedding: frequencies → MLP

Map integer t to a vector of sines and cosines at multiple frequencies, then Linear→SiLU→Linear. Injected into every ResidualBlock so convolution paths know the noise level.

In SD-class U-Nets the sinusoidal block often emits 320 dimensions: half the indices carry sines and half cosines built from geometrically spaced frequencies (same construction spirit as transformer positional encodings, with base 10 000 in reference implementations). That [B,320] vector is then mapped by an MLP to width 1 280—exactly four× the sinusoidal width—before being injected into residual blocks as channel-wise biases after a learned linear projection per block.

f_i = exp(−log(10000) · i / (d/2)),   emb = concat(sin(t f), cos(t f)) ∈ ℝ^{320}

Shape

te is [B, t_dim]; broadcast with [:,:,None,None] over NCHW.

06

ResidualBlock: Conv2d + time bias

Two 3×3 convolutions with GroupNorm and SiLU. time_linear(te) adds channel-wise activation after the first conv. 1×1 skip adjusts channels when in_ch≠out_ch.

Fusing three signals. The denoiser must condition on (i) the noisy latent, (ii) the diffusion index t, and (iii) the text embedding stream. Residual blocks address (i)+(ii) by adding a projected time vector across spatial dimensions—te_proj[:, :, None, None]—after the first normalized convolution so feature maps know both local appearance and global noise level. (iii) is handled separately in AttentionBlock stages via cross-attention, where queries come from spatial features and keys/values from context.

GroupNorm groups. Convolutional features are grouped along the channel axis so statistics are computed within related filter banks—closer to how local receptive fields cluster information than full LayerNorm across all channels at once. Stable Diffusion stacks overwhelmingly use GroupNorm with group counts that divide the channel width (commonly 32 groups when widths are multiples of thirty-two).

Fusion

Same pattern repeats at each resolution inside input_blocks and output_blocks; decoder blocks also receive concatenated skip tensors.

AttentionBlock: self- and cross-attention

Reshape (B,C,H,W) → (B,H·W,C). Self-attention uses Q,K,V from x. Cross-attention uses Q from x and K,V from context (B,77,768 projected to model width). Multi-head scaled_dot_product_attention is the workhorse.

Self-attention on a grid. Treat each spatial location as a token whose “embedding” is its channel vector. Then scaled dot-product attention mixes information across the entire field—any position may attend to any other—complementing convolutions, which remain local unless stacked deeply.

Cross-attention to text. Queries originate from latent tokens; keys and values originate from the prompt tensor [B,77,768] after linear projections to the block width. Unlike CLIP’s causal text stack, no causal mask is applied: every spatial query may attend to every caption token, reflecting that image synthesis is not left-to-right text generation.

Full SD U-Net attention modules often interleave self-attention, cross-attention, and a small feed-forward (“GEGLU-style”) MLP on the sequence, with residual wiring mirroring transformer blocks—implemented efficiently by reshaping NCHW ↔ (B, HW, C) at block boundaries.

Self-attention

Q,K,V all projected from the same spatial feature map; mixes purely visual context across distance.

Cross-attention

Q from visuals, K,V from text embeddings; each local feature decides which words to read for the next denoising move.

Where it sits

Inserted at selected resolutions in encoder and decoder legs plus middle_block for global mixing at bottleneck width.

Downsample, Upsample, SwitchSequential

Downsample: Conv2d(..., stride=2) or pool+conv. Upsample: resize + conv blur. SwitchSequential iterates children: passes (x,te,ctx) to ResidualBlock and AttentionBlock, plain forward(x) for conv-only modules—mirrors how SD stacks heterogeneous layers.

Representative SD1.x U-Net geometry. Latents enter at 4×64×64 for a 512×512 canvas. An initial conv_in widens channels to 320 while keeping 64×64. Repeated stride-2 convolutions step the grid down through 32×32 and 16×16 while channel widths grow toward 640 and 1280. The deepest trunk reaches 8×8 (for 512-pixel inputs) with 1280 channels at the bottleneck. Decoder stages concatenate skip tensors, so the first wide decoder tensor often has 2560 input channels (two× one-thousand-two-hundred-eighty) before convolutions fuse skip and upsampled features back toward 320, after which a final projection head returns four channels—the noise prediction matching latent shape.

Attention widths track channel depth: head counts such as 8 with per-head dimensions 40 or 80 appear at different resolutions so that heads × d_head matches the block’s model dimension after reshaping.

Skips

Store shallow encoder activations; decoder blocks torch.cat along C before the next ResidualBlock.

DDPMScheduler: betas, alphas_cumprod, step

Scaled linear schedule: interpolate √β_start to √β_end, square. Precompute alphas_cumprod for add_noise. step converts ε̂ to predicted z_0, then posterior mean toward t-1. set_strength picks starting index for img2img.

Training assumes T = 1000 discrete noise indices. The variance schedule is a sequence β₁…β_T controlling how much Gaussian noise is injected per forward step. Stable Diffusion’s public configs use a scaled linear recipe: build β_t = ( linear_interp(√β_start, √β_end, t) )², matching the Hugging Face diffusers “scaled_linear” port of the official repository (cosine schedules exist in literature but are not the SD1.x default).

Forward sampling for training pairs uses DDPM equation (4): with α̅_t stored as alphas_cumprod[t],

x_t = √(α̅_t) · x_0 + √(1 − α̅_t) · ε,   ε ~ 𝒩(0, I)

Reverse updates first recover a predicted clean sample x̂_0 from (x_t, ε̂_θ) (paper eq. 15 in Ho et al.), then form the posterior mean for x_{t−1} using coefficients assembled from α_t, α̅_t, and β_t (eqs. 6–7). Optional Gaussian noise (scaled by a variance recipe) is added except at the terminal step where the variance goes to zero. Implementations often follow the coefficient layout from diffusers because the algebra is fixed once β_t is chosen.

Inference typically uses far fewer than one thousand steps: a common choice is 50 substeps spaced across the training index range (step ratio 20 when 1000 / 50), e.g. indices like 999, 979, 959, … down toward 0. Fewer steps trades quality for speed; some samplers (DDIM, solvers) change the trade-off curve, but plain DDPM at ~fifty steps is already usable.

Naming collision. Codebases sometimes call the reverse updater a “scheduler” even though β_t is also a schedule; here set_timesteps builds the inference index list, while betas defines the training-time noise law.

Inference steps

Use set_timesteps(50) to stride through 999…0; fewer steps than training T=1000.

Classifier-free guidance

Train with random null embeddings so ε_θ(z,t,∅) and ε_θ(z,t,c) share weights. Inference: cat batch on z_t, run U-Net once, chunk outputs, combine ε̃ = ε_u + w(ε_c - ε_u). Larger w tightens prompt adherence; extreme values can oversaturate.

Training recipe. With some probability (many public recipes use roughly fifty percent), replace the text conditioner with an empty or null embedding so the same parameters must explain both conditional and unconditional denoising. At sample time you therefore have access to two plausible noise directions for the same z_t.

Inference combination. Stack z_t twice along batch, run the U-Net once with context formed by concatenating the positive and negative prompt embeddings ([2, 77, 768] for batch-one sampling), split outputs with chunk(2, dim=0), then apply

ε̃ = ε_u + w · (ε_c − ε_u)

with w = 1 recovering the unconditional direction only and w > 1 exaggerating the vector from unguided noise prediction toward the guided one. Practical interfaces often label w “CFG scale” and sweep roughly 1…14; 7.5 is a common default. The unconditional branch can carry negative-prompt wording instead of empty text.

Pipeline assembly (tabbed build)

Wire VAE, CLIP, U-Net, scheduler in order: encode prompts, initialize z (random or noised encode), loop timesteps with CFG, decode. That ordering covers text-to-image end-to-end and extends to img2img via strength.

End-to-end text-to-image loop. (1) Tokenize the prompt to length 77 with padding; run CLIP to obtain context. (2) Initialize z as Gaussian noise with latent shape [B,4,H/8,W/8]. (3) For each inference index t, build the sinusoidal time embedding, optionally duplicate latents for CFG, forward the U-Net to predict ε̂, merge conditional and unconditional predictions with scale w, and call scheduler.step to produce a slightly cleaner z. (4) After the last step, pass z through the VAE decoder and map channels from [-1,1] back to [0,255] for storage.

Performance hygiene. Wrap inference in torch.inference_mode(); move CLIP to CPU (“idle device”) once embeddings are computed if GPU memory is tight. Only the diffusion trunk needs to stay resident during the long timestep loop.

Tokenizer boundary. Minimal from-scratch builds still import a BPE tokenizer (vocab.json + merges.txt) because reimplementing byte-pair encoding is orthogonal to diffusion math.

Inpainting and img2img

Img2img: z_0 = encode(image), choose t_start from strength, add_noise, run reverse loop—more noise ⇒ more deviation from source. Inpainting: latent mask m; after each step, z ← m⊙z_pred + (1-m)⊙forward_noised(z_orig) so unmasked regions track the original at every t.

Img2img strength in plain language. After VAE-encoding the source photo, forward-diffuse the latent to a noise level implied by strength ∈ [0,1]. Higher strength injects more noise, freeing the U-Net to hallucinate larger changes; lower strength keeps the trajectory close to the encoded signal so edits stay local. With T=1000 training noise levels and 50 inference steps, a qualitative picture is that strength ≈ 0.6 begins the reverse chain near index ≈600 instead of 999, shortening the remaining denoising work while still conditioning on the prompt at every step.

Pixels to tensors. Load H×W×3 uint8 images, resize to the checkpoint’s expected square (classically 512×512 for SD1.x demos), rescale channel values from [0,255] to [-1,1], then permute to NCHW before the VAE encoder. Reverse the rescale after decoding for visualization.

Inpainting checkpoints. Standard SD1.5 base weights are not specialized for mask-conditioned inputs; released inpainting variants extend the U-Net input channels (commonly 9 = masked RGB + mask + latent) and require matching state dicts. The latent compositing identity z ← m⊙z_new + (1−m)⊙z_known still expresses the user intent—keep known regions on the forward-noise orbit while the network paints inside the mask—but production systems must load weights trained for that interface.

9-channel U-Net (optional)

Some checkpoints concatenate masked RGB and mask as extra conv_in channels for stronger conditioning; the compositing trick still applies to vanilla latents.

File layout and tensor contracts

A from-scratch port usually splits encoder.py, decoder.py, clip.py, attention.py, diffusion.py (U-Net assembly), ddpm.py, and pipeline.py. model_loader.py holds regex or dict renames from checkpoint keys to your nn.Module attribute paths; verify with strict=False first, then tighten to strict=True.

Weight naming reality. Released checkpoints use hierarchical keys (model.diffusion_model.*, first_stage_model.*, cond_stage_model.*, sometimes ema shadow weights). Tutorial code often renames modules for clarity; a large model_converter map is therefore routine—automatic load_state_dict alignment fails unless names match exactly.

Hugging Face hosts both raw training weights and EMA (exponential moving average) smoothed variants; inference typically favors EMA-smoothed tensors for stability, while fine-tuning may target the non-EMA copy. Standard samplers suppress stochastic noise on the last transition so the chain terminates deterministically given ε̂.

  • conv_in.weight on the U-Net expects 4 latent channels (SD1.x); inpaint variants may use 9.
  • context tensor: float32, shape [B, 77, 768], L2-normalized in some training recipes—not always at inference.
  • timestep expanded to [B] integers indexing buffers alphas_cumprod, sqrt_one_minus_alphas_cumprod, etc.
  • CFG doubles B on z and context; split ε before scheduler.step.
